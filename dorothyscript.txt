DOROTHY TOURNAMENT PRESENTATION SCRIPT
Target time: about 3 minutes, including narration over the recorded demo.

Important: Rehearse this exact wording, but do not read it during the presentation. The rubric caps
the verbal category at 3 out of 5 when notes are read. Use each slide as the prompt for its section.


SLIDE 14 OF 33 - A COMPLETE TOURNAMENT, FROM SETUP TO CHAMPION

"Eight Pokemon enter, and one becomes the champion. My user story is: As a Pokemon player, I want
to run an eight-Pokemon knockout tournament so that a four-two-one bracket produces a champion.

This is the before view. The player places official or custom Pokemon in any slot, while question
marks are resolved randomly when the tournament starts.

Watch the question marks as I start the demo. In the during view, four quarterfinal battles are
submitted in parallel. Each match reuses the single-battle rules and creates a fresh Battle, so HP
and battle state reset.

This is the after view. Only after every match finishes do the winners populate the bracket. The
process repeats with two semifinals and one final."


SLIDE 15 OF 33 - ONE INTERACTOR COORDINATES A ROUND

"This is RunTournamentRoundInteractor, my Use Case Interactor. It loads the tournament, runs the
matchups, records the round, saves the aggregate, and sends Output Data through the Output Boundary.

The Interactor coordinates application steps, while the Tournament aggregate entity enforces eight
entrants, four-two-one round sizes, bracket order, and one champion. That separation applies the
Single Responsibility Principle.

The highlighted CompletableFuture call submits one task per matchup. The Executor and
TournamentMatchRunner Strategy are injected, and every future joins before recording. Production
is parallel, while tests use deterministic fakes and a same-thread Executor."


SLIDE 16 OF 33 - FULL USE-CASE ARCHITECTURE DIAGRAM AND DEPENDENCY RULE

"This is the full class diagram. Runtime control crosses the View and Controllers, enters through
the Input Boundary, reaches the Interactor and Entities, then leaves through the Output Boundary.
The Presenter implements that boundary and creates the View Model.

Runtime control and source-code dependency are not the same. The Interactor owns abstractions such
as TournamentDataAccessInterface and TournamentMatchRunner. Outer repositories, gateways, and the
Presenter implement those interfaces, so source-code dependencies point inward.

TournamentMatchRunner is the Strategy pattern. A different runner can be added without modifying
the Interactor, entities, repository, Presenter, or View, demonstrating Open-Closed and Dependency
Inversion. RandomBattleRunner also calls Albert's ResolveTurnInputBoundary, reusing battle rules
instead of duplicating them."


SLIDE 17 OF 33 - DETAILED UML CLASS DIAGRAM

"This second diagram uses conventional UML class notation. Each box separates the class name,
private fields, and public operations. The filled diamonds show composition: one Tournament owns
up to three completed TournamentRounds, and each round owns four, two, or one TournamentMatch.
The multiplicities encode the eight-four-two-one bracket directly.

The dashed hollow-triangle arrows mean implements, just like in the sample UML. The Interactor
implements its Input Boundary, RandomBattleRunner implements the TournamentMatchRunner Strategy,
the Presenter implements the Output Boundary, and InMemoryTournamentDataAccess implements both
the repository and ID-generator interfaces.

There is intentionally no solid inheritance arrow because no tournament class extends a base
class. Our polymorphism comes from interfaces, and our domain structure comes from composition;
adding inheritance just to resemble an example would make the diagram less accurate."
